Architecture

Four shapes to use, four to avoid

With more than two agents, how you arrange them matters more than the messages. Each card says where this lab shows the shape, and where it doesn't.

Pattern 1

A coordinator with specialists

One agent takes the request and hands parts of it to agents that each do one thing. The caller sees one agent and one task.

orchestrator · lesson 12
Pattern 2

A gateway at the door

Sign-in, limits and the version check happen before the agent runs. The agent is told who is calling and never sees the credential.

src/server/http.ts · lessons 8, 13
Pattern 3

Questions travel back up

When a specialist pauses to ask something, the coordinator pauses its own task in the same state and asks its caller. The spec describes this chain for credentials.

not built · exercise 7
Pattern 4

A callback, with a poll to fall back on

For long work the agent posts to your address. Deliveries can be lost or repeated, so confirm the task's state with GetTask before acting.

lessons 7 and 6, not combined
Avoid 1

A2A inside one program

Both agents live in the same program. A function call does the job with no network, no sign-in and nothing extra to run.

this lab does it on purpose, for teaching
Avoid 2

The long chain

Every hop adds delay, cost and a place to fail, and one failure fails everything behind it. Keep chains short, with one time limit for the whole request.

longest chain here: two hops
Avoid 3

Holding the line for hours

Streaming suits minutes. A connection held open for hours drops part-way and leaves you guessing. Use returnImmediately, then a callback or GetTask.

lessons 4 and 6
Avoid 4

Obeying what comes back

A reply from another agent goes into your model as if it were instructions. Whoever controls that agent now steers yours. Treat every reply as data.

concierge, naive mode · lesson 13
